# Maintenance-related SMS

## Principle: when asking for photos, say exactly what to photograph

"Please send photos" is useless — the tenant sends one wide shot taken from the
doorway. You have to name the **specific spots** for the fixture in question.

## What to ask, by fixture

| Report | Spots to photograph | Question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Toilet leaking | Base where it meets the floor, fill valve behind the tank, inside the tank | Does it leak constantly or only after flushing? |
| Sink / pipe leaking | P-trap underneath, the joints, the cabinet floor | Does it still leak with the faucet off? |
| No hot water | Water heater nameplate, pressure gauge, relief valve at the bottom | Is cold water normal? What about other rooms? |
| Heat not working | Radiator valve, boiler pressure gauge | Is the whole unit cold, or just one room? |
| Appliance failure | Nameplate/model, the display panel, the matching breaker | Did a breaker trip? Have you tried another outlet? |
| Ceiling water stain | The point of ingress, the matching spot upstairs | Is it worse when the unit above is using water? |
| Door / window damage | Close-up of the damage, plus the whole door or window | Does it still lock? (this is a security issue) |

## SMS template

> Hi Sarah, thanks for reporting the toilet leak. To get the right parts
> ordered, could you send a couple of photos — the base where it meets the
> floor, and behind the tank where the water line connects? Also, does it
> leak constantly or only after flushing?

Key points:
- Acknowledge the report first; what tenants fear most is not knowing whether
  anyone saw it
- Say **why** you need photos ("to get the right parts ordered") — not as
  bureaucratic process
- At most 2 spots + 1 question. Ask more and nobody replies
- Where video beats photos (leaks, unusual noises), you can add "a short video
  works too"

## Emergencies

When water, electricity, gas, loss of heat, or any safety hazard is involved,
**give the self-help step before asking for photos**:

> Hi Sarah, thanks for the alert. If water is still running, please shut
> off the valve behind the toilet (turn clockwise). Once it's safe, a photo
> of the leak point would help. We're on it.

**The smell of gas is the one case that is «get out first, everything else
later»**, and the wording must override every other consideration:

> If you smell gas, leave the unit now and call 911 from outside.
> Don't switch anything on or off on your way out.

Do not attach any photo request or follow-up question to that one — every extra
word delays the evacuation.

Key points:
- Make the self-help step concrete, down to the direction ("turn clockwise")
- "We're on it" conveys urgency **without promising a time**
- Photos drop to secondary; safety comes first

## Never write

- "A plumber will be there tomorrow" — dispatch is the landlord's decision
- "This is covered by us" / "You'll need to pay for this" — cost allocation is a
  contractual question
- "This is normal wear" — characterizing it can affect a deposit dispute
