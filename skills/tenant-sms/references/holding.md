# Holding replies (acknowledge & hold)

For messages **the system has judged it should not handle itself**: classification
uncertain, anything touching the lease, the deposit, or legal matters, or
anything needing the landlord's own judgment.

## Why this has to exist

Before holding replies existed, the outcome for these messages was: the system
notified the landlord and **the tenant heard nothing at all**.

A tenant sends a text about their deposit and waits two days with no response —
they don't know whether it arrived, or whether anyone is looking. That silence
escalates the conflict by itself, and in a dispute it works against the landlord
("I sent a message and nobody ever replied").

An acknowledgement that commits to nothing is **the safest outbound message this
system can send**: it creates no obligation, yet it removes the silence.

## Hard rules

**This kind of reply does exactly one thing: confirm receipt + say someone will follow up.**

Never:
- Take any position ("that shouldn't be a problem" / "we'll take care of it")
- Promise a timeframe ("within 24 hours" / "by tomorrow")
- Quote or interpret lease terms — the agent does not read the lease, so any
  interpretation may be wrong
- Mention amounts, allocation of responsibility, or legal provisions
- Use words like "unfortunately" / "I'm afraid" that hint at an outcome

**Use "we", not "I".** This is not one person's commitment; it is the property
following up.

## General template

> Hi Sarah, thanks for your message — we've received it and passed it along.
> Someone will review it and get back to you.

Three sentences at most. That is the whole thing.

## Per-situation adjustments

### Classification uncertain (the system can't tell what the message is about)

> Hi Sarah, thanks for reaching out. We've got your message and someone
> will follow up shortly.

Don't reveal that the system didn't understand. The tenant doesn't need to know
the internal mechanics.

### Lease, deposit, renewal, or move-out

> Hi Sarah, thanks for your message. This one needs a closer look —
> we've passed it to the property manager and someone will get back to you.

Key points:
- "needs a closer look" signals it is being taken seriously without hinting at
  an outcome
- Say plainly that it went to a person ("property manager") rather than implying
  the system is handling it
- **Never** respond to the substance, however simple the question looks

### Legal matters, complaints, disputes, or a mention of a lawyer

> Hi Sarah, we've received your message and it's being reviewed.
> We'll follow up with you directly.

Key points:
- The shortest version. Fewer words is better
- No "thanks" — in the context of a complaint it reads as flippant
- Not "passed along" (which implies handing off) but "being reviewed" (which
  implies genuine attention)
- **Never** express agreement, denial, apology, or explanation in any form.
  This text may end up in a legal proceeding, and the only thing it should
  prove is that the message was received and the landlord is dealing with it

### An apparent emergency safety issue described unclearly

> Hi Sarah, we've got your message and we're looking at it now. If this is
> an emergency — fire, gas, or flooding — please call 911 first.

This is the only holding reply that may add substance, because pointing someone
to 911 is zero-risk and may save a life.

## Self-check

1. Does it take a position on the substance? (If so → delete it)
2. Does it promise a time? (If so → delete it)
3. Does it mention lease terms, amounts, or responsibility? (If so → delete it)
4. Three sentences or fewer?
5. If this text were read aloud to a judge, would it put the landlord in a worse
   position?
